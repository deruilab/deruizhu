Software Engineering · Trustworthy AI

Derui Zhu

Engineering efficient, reliable, and private AI-powered software systems.

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Software Engineering at Rochester Institute of Technology (RIT). My research focuses on the reliability of AI-based software systems, including large language models and agentic AI, with an emphasis on security, privacy, and system performance. I collaborate closely with Prof. Weiyi Shang and Prof. Lei Ma.

I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from the Technical University of Munich, supervised by Prof. Jens Grossklags. I previously obtained my M.Sc. from RWTH Aachen University and my B.Eng. from Harbin Institute of Technology.

Before joining RIT, I was a postdoctoral researcher in Prof. Alexander Pretschner’s group at the Technical University of Munich. My industry experience at JD.COM and Orange Business informs my interest in building AI systems that work reliably in practice.

Research

My research focuses on engineering efficient, reliable, and private AI-powered software systems. I develop methods to audit generative models for privacy and reliability, and use AI to improve software performance. These goals connect two research directions:

  • Privacy & reliability of AI systems

    I study how AI systems expose sensitive data or produce unreliable behavior, using privacy auditing, behavioral analysis, and model-based testing.

    • Privacy auditing and data protection: IPAuditor [TDSC ’26], PrivAuditor [NeurIPS ’24 · Spotlight], and DeepMemory [ASE ’21]; data protection in vertical federated learning [TIFS ’24] [ESORICS ’24].
    • Hallucination detection and safeguards: HD-NDEs [ACL ’25], PoLLMgraph [NAACL ’24], reference-free detection for vision-language models [EMNLP ’24], and internal activation revision [AAAI ’25].
    • Model-based analysis and testing: LUNA [TSE ’24], neural episodic control with state abstraction [ICLR ’23 · Spotlight], and generative model-based testing [ASE ’23].

  • AI for software performance

    I combine LLM-based code generation and automated microbenchmarking with empirical analysis of performance bugs to improve software efficiency and detect regressions reliably.

    • Efficient code generation: LLM-as-a-Critique [NeurIPS ’25].
    • Performance testing and analysis: Stop When It Matters [ASE ’26] and LLM4JMH [ICSE ’26]; an empirical study of performance bugs in the Linux kernel [JSS ’26].
